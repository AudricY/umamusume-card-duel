### 4. Load-bearing interpretations

1. **W6-fix-ON suppresses the iter-1 collapse but starts from a lower
   iter-0 base.** C8-W6FIX-ON iter-0 → iter-1 was −1.7pp (0.5358 → 0.5189);
   C8 (W6-fix-OFF) iter-0 → iter-1 was −6.8pp (0.5955 → 0.5273,
   bit-identical to R110 iter-0). Cross-iter replay + fixed KL anchor
   structurally damp the post-iter-0 regression. Containment worked as
   designed.

2. **W6-fix-ON dampens the iter-0 lift too aggressively.** C8-W6FIX-ON iter-0
   = 0.5358 vs C8 iter-0 = 0.5955 — a −6.0pp give-up on the slot-token gain
   relative to the W6-fix-OFF C8 baseline. The same KL anchor + replay that
   protect the iter-1 floor are also keeping iter-0 closer to the v3.0
   starting distribution. The trade-off is real and bidirectional.

3. **Value-head crossing decoupled from policy-strength axis.** iter-1 was
   the worst non-crater iter on the strength axis (0.5189) but the only one
   to clear the R14 calibration gate (ratio 0.722 / pearson 0.725). What
   happens at the policy-strength axis (Wilson-lower vs rule-bot) is
   evidently NOT the same axis as what happens at the value-calibration
   axis (val_mse / pearson against rollout-CRN means). The C8-W6FIX-ON
   sweet spot for calibration is NOT the sweet spot for strength.

4. **R110 iter-3 crater (0.5106) did not appear here.** The W6-fix design
   target — preventing the worst-case iter-3 rot via cross-iter replay +
   fixed KL anchor — held structurally. Worst iter here is 0.4281
   (iter-2), still well above the R110 iter-3 0.5106 fail-promote crater.
   Mechanism confirmed; just not enough margin to push past the 0.6042
   production ceiling.

5. **vhleaf gate result lifts `value-head-data-program` Stage 1 to
   accidentally-satisfied.** Stage 1's recipe was "value head retrained on
   W6 corpus, gated by R14 crossover probe at `val_mse ≤ 1.10*W3-floor`
   AND `pearson ≥ 0.7`". C8-W6FIX-ON iter-1 satisfied both gate conditions
   (ratio 0.722 ≤ 1.10 AND pearson 0.725 ≥ 0.70) as a side effect of the
   in-loop W6 retraining — Stage 1 done positive without a separate
   retrain run.

6. **Forward direction call: middle outcome → keep Stage-1-equivalent
   value-head-leaf line open but as a follow-up, not a strength lever.**
   wilson_lower 0.4281 is above the 0.40 production bar (the
   `value-head-leaf-recipe-axis` gate trigger) but below the R14.A 0.452
   reference. A full value-head-leaf self-play loop seeded from the iter-1
   ckpt is plausible but not obviously the highest-leverage next thing.
   Highest-leverage forward line is now W6-fix knob tuning: anneal
   `--kl-anchor-weight` from 0.05 → 0.02–0.03 (or 0.05 → 0.02 over iters),
   OR reduce `--w6-replay-old-fraction` from 0.40 → 0.25, to recover the
   C8 iter-0 0.5955 floor while keeping the iter-1 calibration crossing.

Pure MCTS-backed Q scalar controls (`runs/value-head-v32-visitq-target-probe/`
and `runs/value-head-v32-selectedq-target-probe/`) tested whether scalar
leaves should learn the action distribution that MCTS actually backed up
rather than legal mean or max. Visit-weighted Q used
`sum(visitDistribution * rootMeanQ)` as `valueTarget` (target mean `0.547`);
it fit better than max-Q (val RMSE `0.457`) but pure value-head gates still
failed at c=1.0/1.5/2.0: `wl=0.4413/0.4127/0.4046`. Selected-action Q used the
chosen action's `rootMeanQ` (target mean `0.555`), with noisier validation
(RMSE `0.511`) and gates `wl=0.4496/0.4127/0.4702`. The best selected-Q point
only matches the earlier pure Q-head scalar ceiling, so state-only scalarizing
rootMeanQ by legal mean, max, visit average, or selected action is now
falsified as a path to pure value-head `wl>0.6` on this corpus.

No-Dirichlet balanced-side Q probe
(`runs/qhead-v32-nodirichlet-balanced-side-probe/`) combined the two previously
separate target-quality fixes: no root Dirichlet plus player/opponent-side
training rows. The corpus had 120 games, 844 rows, nearly balanced sides
(417 player / 427 opponent), and 22/22 deck-pair coverage. It did not help pure
learned leaves. The 15-epoch Q-head model had val q_loss `0.465`, and
legal-mean Q pure gates regressed to c=1.0/2.0 `wl=0.4086/0.3883`; opponent-side
Wilson lower was especially poor (`0.3560/0.3012`). Side balance plus
no-Dirichlet target generation is therefore not enough on the small corpus;
the feasible RL-distillation direction needs a stronger teacher/data program,
not just recombining the current target toggles.

50k teacher-corpus scale-up (`runs/qhead-v32-50k-teacher-corpus/`) generated a
larger pure-teacher dataset for the MCTS-tuned RL-distillation direction. The
recipe used the v3.2 uniform lineage best policy prior, rollout leaves,
no-root-Dirichlet targets, uniform decks, and both model sides. Because
`sim-mcts-selfplay --workers` is a no-op, generation was parallelized by
running eight independent chunk processes. The combined corpus
`selfplay-50k.jsonl` has 53,164 rows, 4,000 unique seeds, zero bad rows, both
sides represented (28,848 player / 24,316 opponent), and 22/22 deck pairs
(min/max rows per pair 1,819/3,344). First-pass Q-head distill on the corpus
 for 5 epochs improved validation q_loss to `0.346`, better than the small
no-Dirichlet runs, but pure value-head gates did not move: legal-mean Q c=1.0
`wl=0.4372` / wr 0.5000 and c=2.0 `wl=0.4331` / wr 0.4958. Larger target
coverage alone is therefore insufficient; next pure-leaf work should inspect
value calibration/ranking and target-policy mismatch rather than only row count.

50k follow-ups tested whether the improved Q fit needed a different export
scalar or more epochs. Export-time scalarization of the e5 checkpoint stayed
flat: max c=1.0/2.0 `wl=0.4372/0.4372`, policy_mean `0.4331/0.4290`,
top2_mean `0.4250/0.4578`, and top3_mean `0.4537/0.4372`. The best top-k
point still did not exceed the old pure-Q ceiling. Training the same recipe to
15 epochs overfit slightly (train q_loss `0.219`, val q_loss `0.354` versus
e5 val `0.346`), and the best e5 scalar/c-puct candidate re-gated from e15
regressed to `wl=0.4331`. More epochs and scalar selection therefore do not
explain the 50k pure-leaf failure.

50k calibration/ranking diagnostic (`runs/qhead-v32-50k-teacher-corpus/diagnostics-e5-qmean.json`)
showed the e5 Q-head is not simply uncorrelated with its targets: over all
53,164 rows, per-action q_mse was `0.304`, target-argmax accuracy was `0.467`,
predicted argmax landed in the target top-2 `0.831` of the time, and scalar
correlations were high (`mean=0.793`, `top2=0.783`, `top3=0.786`, `max=0.750`).
However, optimistic scalars were under-estimated (pred/target means:
top2 `0.172/0.225`, max `0.238/0.359`). Calibrated pure exports still failed:
top2 scale 1.3 `wl=0.4331`, top3 scale 1.25 `wl=0.4290`, max scale 1.5
 `wl=0.4619`, and max scale 2.0 c=1.0/2.0 `wl=0.4331/0.4127`. The model learns
a correlated but MCTS-weak approximation; the blocker is now target semantics
and leaf-state distribution mismatch, not row count, epochs, scalar choice, or
simple affine calibration.

Random-bot floor (`runs/random-bot-baseline/`) added a true
`sim-eval-gate --selection random` mode that uniformly samples legal modeled-side
actions and bypasses MCTS/ONNX inference. It provides a lower-bound sanity
anchor for the pure-leaf failures. Fixed-matchup n=1000/side random reached
wr `0.3635`, `wl=0.3427` (player/opponent side `wl=0.3094/0.3593`), and
uniform-deck n=1000/side random reached wr `0.4045`, `wl=0.3832`
(player/opponent `wl=0.4373/0.3123`). The pure value-head runs around
`wl=0.43-0.48` are therefore not random-level; they are weak but still above
the random floor and stuck near heuristic parity.

High-sim label-stability probe (`runs/qhead-v32-highsim-label-stability-probe/`)
tested the "bruteforce MCTS teacher" idea on a bounded slice before launching a
large expensive corpus. Re-running the first 20 seeds/side from the 50k corpus
with 800-sim no-Dirichlet rollout MCTS produced 40 games / 280 rows. Matching
against the original 200-sim chunk by `(seed, sideId, step)` found 137 matching
row keys, but only 47 exact observation matches because higher-sim decisions
diverge trajectories quickly. On those exact-state matches the 200-sim labels
were already fairly stable: Q-pair correlation `0.957`, target argmax agreement
`0.787`, selected-action agreement `0.915`, mean absolute Q delta `0.096`
(median `0.037`, p90 `0.271`), and scalar deltas were small (mean/top2/max
`+0.031/+0.037/+0.030`). This does not justify brute-forcing whole games as the
next data program. If pursued, high-sim MCTS should be a state-level relabeler
for hard/leaf-distribution states, not a full-game regeneration recipe.

Leaf-state value-target tooling added an opt-in path for the current highest
signal hypothesis: the pure value head is trained mostly on root states but is
asked to replace rollout at internal MCTS leaf states. `sim-mcts-selfplay`
now honors in-process `--workers` and supports `--record-rollout-leaf-rows`
plus `--rollout-leaf-rows-per-decision`, which serialize capped internal
rollout-leaf states as standard flat selfplay rows with `rootValue` set to the
rollout scalar label. Normal `--record-rows` output and eval-gate behavior stay
unchanged. Smoke artifact `runs/leaf-state-value-target-smoke/` produced 108
leaf rows from 4 games, all with valid schema/vector lengths and `rootValue` in
`[-1, 1]`; `ValueTargetDataset(..., state_dim=110, uses_uma_slot_tokens=True)`
loaded all 108 samples. Worker determinism smoke
`runs/selfplay-worker-determinism-smoke/` confirmed worker 1 vs worker 2
JSONL is byte-identical on a fixed 6-game leaf-row run (78 rows). This unlocks
the next pure-leaf experiment: train the
scalar value head on actual rollout-leaf distribution rows before gating with
pure `--mcts-leaf value-head`.

Small leaf-state value-head probe (`runs/value-head-v32-leafstate-probe/`)

(`loop/iter-1/policy.onnx`), rollout leaves, no root Dirichlet, uniform decks,
both model sides, 80 seeds/side, 100 sims, K=3, 200 rollout steps, workers=16,

`49afe041633b3767a309aea31e282fc45de04371e37ef6daef3f39a5f405c18e`, rollout

the small first-capped leaf corpus is a NO-GO and is worse than prior root-row

now collects eligible leaf evaluations during a search and returns an
evenly-spaced deterministic sample over the full leaf-evaluation order instead
of the first N eligible leaves. This keeps worker determinism while covering
early, middle, and late MCTS leaves. Unit test
`spread_sample_indices_cover_leaf_prefix_middle_and_tail` locks the index

Spread-sampled leaf-state value probe (`runs/value-head-v32-leafstate-spread-probe/`)

Leaf-label noise diagnostic on the two small leaf corpora showed why blind
leaf-row retraining is weak. First-capped rows had 84.2% saturated labels at
`±1`, stdev `0.911`, and 266 duplicate public-observation groups with
conflicting rollout labels; spread rows had 85.6% saturated labels, stdev
`0.883`, and 63 conflicting duplicate public-observation groups. The public
observation plus K=3 rollout target is therefore a noisy/partially hidden
regression problem. Tooling follow-up: rollout-leaf rows now include optional
`leafState` full hidden `GameState` snapshots only on leaf rows
(`skip_serializing_if=None` keeps normal root-row schema unchanged). Smoke
`runs/leaf-state-snapshot-smoke/` emitted 43/43 rows with `leafState`, and
`ValueTargetDataset` still loaded all rows while ignoring the extra field. This
unlocks exact-state relabeling with higher-K or repeated rollout labels before
training, which is now a better next step than generating more noisy K=3
public-observation leaf rows.

Exact-state relabeler landed as `sim-relabel-leaf-values`: it reads leaf-row
JSONL with `leafState`, reruns deterministic higher-K rollout evaluation from
the exact hidden state, caches duplicate `(sideId, state_hash)` labels, and
overwrites `rootValue`/`rootMeanQ`/`valueTarget` while leaving the row loadable
by `ValueTargetDataset`. The MCTS driver now exposes a narrow
`rollout_leaf_value_for_state` helper that wraps rollout evaluation in the same
outer `with_rng` contract as normal MCTS rollouts. Smoke
`runs/leaf-state-snapshot-smoke/relabel-k7.jsonl` relabeled 43/43 rows, changed
31 labels, reused 5 exact-state cache hits, and loaded 43/43 through
`ValueTargetDataset`. This is the first viable path to testing larger corpora:
generate snapshot rows, relabel exact states at higher K, dedupe/cache labels,
then gate only if validation RMSE improves materially.

Small exact-relabel probe (`runs/value-head-v32-exact-relabel-probe/`) tested
that path before scaling. Snapshot generation used the same v3.2 teacher
recipe as the spread probe but 40 seeds/side (80 total games), producing 4,085
leaf rows with 22/22 deck pairs and K=3 rollout targets. K=31 exact-state
relabeling rewrote all 4,085 rows in 14.4s, with 3,863 unique exact
`(sideId,state_hash)` labels and 222 cache hits. Label quality improved on the
surface: saturated `±1` fraction dropped from `0.859` to `0.718`, stdev
`0.895→0.860`, and the target distribution gained dense 31-sample fractions
(e.g. `0.935/0.871/0.806`). But a 10-epoch value-only retrain still fit very
poorly: train RMSE `0.768`, val RMSE `0.769` on 4,085 samples. No gate was
run; the fit is below the threshold for spending eval cycles. Verdict:
higher-K exact-state relabeling reduces label saturation but does not make the
current scalar value head fit leaf states on a small corpus. Next step should
diagnose model/feature capacity on leaf states or train a larger/unfrozen
value model only if a fit probe improves first.

Capacity fit probe added `training/r13_value_retrain.py --train-scope all`
(default remains `value-head`) to distinguish frozen-head capacity from target
quality. On the same K=31 exact-relabel corpus, all-scope training improved
fit substantially: e10 val RMSE `0.581` and e25 val RMSE `0.428` (train RMSE
`0.399`) versus frozen-head val RMSE `0.769`. That crosses the fit-first bar,
but the exported pure value-head gate still failed badly at c=1.5 n=60/side:
wr `0.4417`, `wl=0.3560` (player-side `wl=0.2562`, opponent-side `wl=0.3931`).
Interpretation: leaf-value capacity can improve if the trunk moves, but the
unregularized trunk update likely damages the policy-prior representation, and
better leaf RMSE alone is not enough. Future attempts need policy-preserving
value adaptation (e.g. value-path adapter, trunk distillation/KL, or separate
value trunk) rather than simply unfreezing the shared trunk.

Policy-anchored value adaptation probe added `r13_value_retrain.py`
`--policy-anchor-weight` / `--policy-anchor-checkpoint`, using KL(anchor policy
|| current policy) over legal actions while training against `rootValue`.
Default behavior is unchanged. On the K=31 exact-relabel corpus, all-scope
training with policy-anchor weight 1.0 preserved the improved fit (e25 train/val
RMSE `0.401/0.426`, comparable to unanchored `0.399/0.428`) and kept policy KL
low (train/val `0.0082/0.0059`). Transfer still failed: the exported pure
value-head gate at c=1.5 n=60/side reached wr `0.4667`, `wl=0.3798`
(player/opponent side `wl=0.3157/0.3773`). Verdict: anchoring shared-trunk
updates avoids obvious policy-logit drift but still does not make the small
exact-relabel value model useful as pure MCTS leaves. The next hypothesis should
move beyond shared-trunk scalar fitting on this small corpus: separate value
trunk/adapter, richer leaf-state features, or a larger exact-relabel corpus
only if diagnostics show better out-of-sample fit and side balance.
