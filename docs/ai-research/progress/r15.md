**Cheap-inference envelope reconciliation.** Value-head-leaf's documented
0.39–0.45 envelope is three independent seed ranges: F-iter-2 0.443
(820000+), R14.A 0.404 (800000+), R14.A.footnote 0.394 (900000+). The
adaptive-ratio=1.5 cell sits at the **top** of that band (0.4524) but the
honest envelope-low is the R14.A.footnote 0.394 — **1-of-3 below the 0.40
production bar**. Value-head-leaf is therefore *at* the bar, not safely
above it; rollout-leaf clears the same bar by ~25pp aggregate.

**Recommended production config: rollout-leaf @ W6 iter-2.** Rationale,
tradeoff stated explicitly:

- **Strength dominates by ~20pp aggregate Wilson lower** (0.6479 vs
  best-case value-head 0.4524, vs honest-envelope-low 0.394). Even
  rollout-leaf's *player-side floor* (0.5577) is above value-head's
  *aggregate best case* (0.4524). This is the decisive factor.
- **Latency is the cost paid.** Rollout-leaf is ~2.5× slower
  (value-head-leaf cuts 61% wall-clock; ~0.5s/decision vs rollout's
  multi-second decisions). For an asynchronous / turn-based card game with
  no hard real-time SLA, a few-second decision is acceptable; the strength
  delta is not worth trading for sub-second latency. If a future hard
  latency SLA emerges (e.g. live multiplayer with a move clock),
  value-head-leaf + ratio=1.5 is the documented fallback — but it must be
  shipped with the honest "*at* the 0.40 bar, 1-of-3 seed ranges below"
  caveat, not as an equivalent.
- **Robustness ties favor rollout-leaf:** 0 fallbacks both configs; both
  deterministic; rollout-leaf additionally has the single-worker CRN
  determinism PASS on the exact production config.

**Honest production claim string (chosen config).**

> *"Search-wrapped rollout-leaf MCTS @ W6 iter-2 (sims=100, rollout-CRN×3,
> policy prior) wins ≥64.8% vs the rule bot (Wilson-95 lower, n=120,
> side-balanced, single-worker-reproducible). The result is side-asymmetric:
> opponent-side ≥66.4%, player-side only ≥55.8% (Wilson-95 lower), a
> ~10pp directional gap (CIs overlap at n=60/side). 0.6479 is an aggregate
> side-balanced Wilson-lower claim, not a per-side or worst-case guarantee."*

**Asymmetry stance: disclose-as-aggregate with the documented per-side
caveat.** This is the default and the evidence supports it as defensible,
not a punt:

- The gap is **directional, not statistically separated** at the available
  n (player upper 0.7869 ≥ opponent lower 0.6638; CIs overlap on both
  configs). A side-conditioned policy would be engineering against a
  not-yet-separated effect.
- The gap is **already quantified** in the claim string; disclosure is
  honest and needs zero extra engineering.
- **Config choice does NOT cleanly shrink the gap.** Rollout-leaf +10.0pp;
  value-head-leaf +6.0pp (ratio-1.5) but +14.0pp (R14.A) — value-head's
  apparently-smaller gap is within seed noise and reverses across seed
  ranges. Switching config to reduce asymmetry is not supported; the gap is
  a property of the position/side, not the leaf evaluator. Both directions
  agree (player weaker), which strengthens "disclose" over "re-architect".
- A side-conditioned policy (e.g. extra sims when modelSide=player) is a
  reasonable **forward bet, not a deployment blocker** — it would need a
  larger-n side-split gate to separate the CIs before it is worth the
  engineering. Logged as forward work, not escalated as a human-owned
  research-stance call: the default disclose stance is fully defensible on
  the current evidence.

**No human research-stance call required.** The config pick (rollout-leaf)
and the disclose-as-aggregate stance are both defensible self-directed
defaults on the assembled evidence. The only judgement deferred — a
side-conditioned sim budget — is explicitly a forward optimisation gated on
a larger side-split sample, not a blocker and not a stance the human must
arbitrate now.
