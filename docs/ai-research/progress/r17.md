## Verdict (2026-05-29)

The ReBeL self-play loop runs **end-to-end** (self-play → train → export → gate →
promote) but does **NOT self-improve**. Two *distinct* problems were found and
separated:

1. **The promotion gate was measuring the wrong thing — FIXED.** R17/R18 judged
   promotion by *absolute win-rate vs a fixed rules-based heuristic* on a
   zero-margin monotone ratchet. The heuristic **saturates ~0.60**, so a stronger
   policy doesn't register a higher number, and the ratchet promoted/blocked on
   noise. Replaced with a **head-to-head challenger-vs-champion gate** (commit
   `430be83`).

2. **With honest gating, the loop has no improvement source — OPEN.** Under the
   head-to-head gate (R19), no trained generation beat iter-0. Worse: as training
   fit the self-play targets better each iteration (**val_acc 0.522 → 0.638**),
   actual strength *fell* (**uniform-vs-heuristic 0.594 → 0.568**; h2h-vs-iter-0
   flat at ~0.49). The two are **anti-correlated** — distilling more of the ReBeL
   search policy makes the model play slightly *worse*. The bottleneck is
   **distillation-target quality**, not the gate.

Net: "loop working e2e" ✅; "self-improving" ❌, now for a well-localized reason.

## 2. Problem 1 — the old promotion gate (saturating absolute + ratchet under noise)

`rebel_orchestrator.py` promoted iff `fixed_gate.passed AND wilson_lower >= best_ever_promoted`,
where `wilson_lower` is the challenger's *absolute* win-rate vs the rules-based
heuristic opponent (`sim-eval-gate` plays MCTS-model vs heuristic). Two failure
modes, both observed:

- **Saturation.** Fixed-deck win-rate clusters ~0.57–0.64 across wildly different
  particle/search/KL settings (R17 0.573–0.642; R18 0.535–0.562). The heuristic
  caps achievable win-rate near ~0.60, so genuine policy gains stop moving the
  number — the improvement signal vanishes.
- **Ratchet under noise.** The `>=` floor has no significance margin and only
  ratchets up. At ~480–960 games the Wilson half-width is ±0.03–0.06, so a model
  statistically *tied* with the incumbent fails ~half the time, and a lucky-high
  promotion locks in a floor later iters can't clear. R17 blocked iters 2–17
  (16 straight non-promotions) despite all passing the absolute bar; R18's
  iter-4 "promotion" was almost certainly a noise blip, not real improvement.

This matches the archived reviewer note "promotion ratchet under noise"
(`docs/archive/ai-research/ai-performance-research-backlog-archive.md`), whose
prescribed fix (confidence-band tolerance) was never implemented — superseded
here by the head-to-head gate.

## 3. Fix — head-to-head challenger-vs-champion gate (commit `430be83`)

The statistically correct test of "did we improve" is: **does the new model beat
the current champion?** This is self-scaling (the opponent strengthens with every
promotion, so no saturation ceiling) and needs no arbitrary ratchet.

Mechanism (4 files, +224/−11):

- **`engine-rs/.../inference/mod.rs`** — added a thread-local session override
  (`set_active_override` + `active()` = override-or-`global()`). `global()`
  unchanged; default behavior byte-for-byte identical for all existing callers.
- **`engine-rs/.../mcts/driver.rs`** — the 3 MCTS model-access sites use
  `inference::active()` instead of `global()`.
- **`engine-rs/.../bin/eval_gate.rs`** — new `--opponent-onnx`: loads a 2nd ONNX
  session and runs the opponent side through `run_mcts` under the override.
  Win attribution / Wilson summary unchanged — challenger is `model_side`, so
  `summary.overall.wilson95.lower` already means "challenger beat champion."
- **`training/rebel_orchestrator.py`** — new `--gate-promote-margin`. The FIXED
  gate runs head-to-head vs `state.promoted_onnx` with `--min-ci-lower = 0.5 +
  margin` when a champion exists; iter-0 bootstraps against the heuristic at
  `--gate-min-ci-lower`. The monotone `wilson_lower >= previous` ratchet was
  **removed**: `promote = gate.passed`. UNIFORM gate stays absolute-vs-heuristic
  as a regression sanity signal. Decision reasons:
  `bootstrap-gate-passed/failed`, `h2h-beat-champion/below-champion`.

**Validation (CPU, 120 games, iter-0/iter-3 of R18):** routing is provably
correct and deterministic — A-vs-B and B-vs-A win-rates are *exactly*
complementary (58/120 vs 62/120) and a model-vs-itself mirror is exactly 60/120.

## 4. R19 result — the null self-improvement finding

Head-to-head gate, p64/s48, KL 0.02, gate 480 games/side, margin 0.0.

| iter | h2h win-rate vs iter-0 | uniform win-rate vs heuristic | final val_acc | decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | — (bootstrap 0.621 vs heuristic) | 0.594 | 0.522 | **promoted → champion** |
| 1 | 0.489 | 0.581 | 0.572 | rejected (h2h-below-champion) |
| 2 | 0.494 | 0.579 | 0.629 | rejected |
| 3 | 0.491 | 0.568 | 0.638 | rejected |

Killed at iter-4 (consec_fail 3/6). **Champion never advanced past iter-0.**

Two robust signals:

- **h2h vs iter-0 is flat at 0.489 / 0.494 / 0.491** — three independent
  960-game measurements clustered within ±0.3% just *below* 50%. Not noise: the
  trained generations are reproducibly even-to-marginally-worse than iter-0. The
  gate correctly refuses to promote them (and was *not* fooled the way the old
  absolute gate was — contrast R18 "promoting" iter-4).
- **val_acc ↑ while strength ↓** — fitting the self-play targets better
  (0.522 → 0.638) tracks *lower* real win-rate (0.594 → 0.568). The distillation
  target is **misaligned with playing strength**: more imitation of the ReBeL
  search policy = slightly weaker play.

## 5. Diagnosis — improvement source, not the gate

The loop's premise is "search > policy, training distills the gain." R19 shows
that pipeline is not delivering a gain, and the val_acc↑/winrate↓ divergence says
the targets themselves are the problem. Compounding it is a **self-play
cold-start**: because nothing beats iter-0, the actor and the entire PFSP pool
stay frozen at iter-0, so every iteration trains on the same iter-0-distribution
data and lands back at ~iter-0. The first improvement is the hardest and the loop
never gets it.

Candidate root causes (unverified, ordered by suspicion):

1. **Search is not stronger than the raw policy at the source.** If iter-0's
   search-driven play does not beat iter-0's raw-policy play, there is no gain to
   distill. This is the decisive check — see next steps.
2. **Value/Q heads corrupting the shared trunk.** `--value-weight 0.2
   --q-value-weight 0.35` on a shared trunk; rising val_acc may be the value/Q
   objectives dragging the policy. A policy-only (value/Q weight 0) iteration
   isolates this.
3. **KL-anchor-to-iter-0 pinning** the learner (init-from-iter-0 + KL anchor +
   gate-vs-iter-0 all point at iter-0), suppressing any move away from it.
4. **Too little data per iter** (768 games, ~5–8k rows) to extract a small
   search edge.

## 8. Correctness audit of the ReBeL loop (2026-05-29)

Full audit of search + training (Rust `rebel/mod.rs`, `rebel_selfplay.rs`; Python
`rebel_orchestrator.py`, `train_bc.py`, `rebel_dataset.py`). The `b07c2d3` fixes
hold: value head trains on the grounded MC `valueTarget` not `beliefValue`; value
perspective is consistently observer-side across rows; neural-leaf default +
loud-fail paths present; gate ratchet removed; replay drops `rolloutLeafUsed`/
missing-vintage old rows; temperature actually scales. The null result (§4) is
explained by **three compounding defects in the distillation target itself**,
which confirm root-cause #1 ("search is not stronger than the raw policy at the
source"):

**A. The "search" is one-ply PIMC, not CFR or multi-ply lookahead (structural).**
`run_public_belief_search` advances only the observer's own action one ply
(`rebel/mod.rs:169-176`), neural-evaluates the resulting state, and averages over
determinized particles. `max_depth` is declared but never read (`rebel/mod.rs:25,45`
only). `average_strategy_from_values` runs regret-matching over a *constant* value
vector (`rebel/mod.rs:389-427`), which provably collapses to a smooth argmax — the
code's own "HONEST LABELING" comment (`rebel/mod.rs:290-298`) states it is not
equilibrium CFR and the `public-belief-cfr-v1` wire string is schema-continuity
only. There is no opponent decision node. **Consequence:** the search policy is
essentially the value net read greedily at depth 1, blended with the policy net;
it has no mechanism to be systematically stronger than the raw policy, so there is
no gain to distill. Deepest cause of the null result.

**B. Active bug — turn-ending actions get leaf value exactly 0 (anti-pass bias).**
When the observer's action passes the turn (non-terminal `next`, no observer legal
actions), the neural leaf is skipped (`rebel/mod.rs:205-220`) and aggregation falls
back to the rollout value, which is `0.0` when rollouts are off
(`rebel/mod.rs:193-197, 264-271`). So turn-ending actions get Q≈0 across all
particles while turn-keeping actions get the (often positive) neural value — the
distilled `searchPolicy` is biased against ending the turn for reasons unrelated to
value. The policy head learns this biased argmax (val_acc ↑) while real strength
falls. Fix: evaluate the leaf from the mover's perspective and sign-flip to the
observer (minimal), or add a real opponent node / depth-2 search (scoped change).
OPEN.

**C. R19's evidence is contaminated — re-qualify §4.** `runs/launch-R19-rebel-h2h.sh:30`
ran self-play with `--neural-leaf-weight 0.8` and no `--allow-rollout-leaf`, i.e. a
20% info-incorrect rollout-leaf blend. The `b07c2d3` guard that now rejects `<1.0`
without the flag (`rebel_orchestrator.py:909-915`) landed after R19. The headline
"val_acc↑/strength↓" was therefore measured on data current code forbids; a
`--neural-leaf-weight 1.0` rerun is required before treating "distillation target
misaligned" as settled.

Secondary (correct-but-contributing): init-from-iter-0 + fixed-KL-anchor-to-iter-0
(`--fixed-kl-anchor` default true, `rebel_orchestrator.py:703-712`) + gate-vs-iter-0
+ PFSP-pool-{iter-0} all pin the learner to iter-0 (cold-start trap, §5). Value/Q
share the trunk with no stop-grad (`model.py:706-744`); with R19's `--value-weight
0.2 --q-value-weight 0.35` those gradients can degrade the policy representation — a
stop-grad ablation is the cheap check. Note `val_acc` is hard top-1 vs
`selectedActionIndex` while the loss is soft-CE vs the full `searchPolicy`
(`train_bc.py:825-827, 1051`); it is a side metric, not a fit proxy.

Priority: fix B (active bug), then rerun the §6 search-vs-policy probe at leaf=1.0
to settle whether A is fatal, then the KL-off / stop-grad ablations.

## 9. Resolution — search rewritten as a policy-improvement rollout (2026-05-29)

A/B/C from §8 are addressed by a rewrite of the Rust public-belief search, not a
patch. `run_public_belief_search` (`engine-rs/crates/engine/src/rebel/mod.rs`) now
evaluates each root action with a **turn-aware policy-improvement rollout**: apply
the action to each weighted particle, then continue the modeled game forward under
the network's own greedy policy (each mover acting on its *own* observation) for up
to `max_depth` modeled-turn steps, and bootstrap the leaf with the network value
sign-converted to the observer. Averaging over particles yields one public
action-value vector; the root policy is a temperature softmax over it (replacing the
regret-matching-over-a-constant-vector collapse).

- **A (no gain source) → addressed.** This is one step of policy iteration (evaluate
  `Q^pi(s,a)`, act greedily), a genuine improvement operator over the raw net.
  `max_depth` is now read and meaningful (was dead). It remains determinized PIMC,
  not equilibrium CFR — honestly relabeled `public-belief-policy-improvement-v2`;
  full belief-CFR stays the tracked long-term target in `rebel-e2e-scoping.md`.
- **B (zero-leaf anti-pass bias) → fixed.** The rollout continues through the turn
  handover and evaluates the opponent's resulting position (negated), so turn-ending
  actions are valued by their continuation, never silently 0. A `degenerate_leaf_count`
  diagnostic + unit test guard against any silent dead-end-to-0.
- **C (info-incorrect leaf contamination) → removed at the source.** The heuristic
  rollout leaf and the `neural_leaf_weight`/`allow_rollout_leaf`/`rollout_steps` knobs
  are deleted; the network/observation value is the only leaf. `--onnx-path` is now
  mandatory (the binary exits without one). `rolloutLeafUsed` stays in the row schema,
  always false, so the replay-vintage filter keeps working.
- **V1 info-set invariance preserved:** each rollout's RNG is seeded from the
  particle's content-stable label, so the root policy is invariant to particle
  ordering (unit-tested) and independent of how chance resolves.

Tests: engine `rebel::` 3/3, sim-cli `sim-rebel-selfplay` 1/1, Python rebel smokes
PASS. **Throughput follow-up (landed):** lockstep wave-batching of the in-tree
rollouts shipped in this same rewrite — `run_public_belief_search` carries a
`Vec<Rollout>` advanced in lockstep and `advance_rollout_wave` issues one batched
`predict_v3_batch_with_belief` per depth wave (NOT per-step single predicts). The
remaining throughput characterization (inference-bound; batch-size and worker
sweeps are null for the 0.75M relational model; within-wave eval dedup) lives in
`docs/ai-research/scoping/rebel-throughput-optimization-slice.md`.
