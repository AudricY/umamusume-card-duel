## Verdict (2026-05-29)

The ReBeL self-play loop runs **end-to-end** (self-play → train → export → gate →
promote) but does **NOT self-improve**. Two *distinct* problems were found and
separated:

1. **The promotion gate was measuring the wrong thing — FIXED.** R17/R18 judged
   promotion by *absolute win-rate vs a fixed rules-based heuristic* on a
   zero-margin monotone ratchet. The heuristic **saturates ~0.60**, so a stronger
   policy doesn't register a higher number, and the ratchet promoted/blocked on
   noise. Replaced with a **head-to-head challenger-vs-champion gate** (commit
   `430be83`).

2. **With honest gating, the loop has no improvement source — OPEN.** Under the
   head-to-head gate (R19), no trained generation beat iter-0. Worse: as training
   fit the self-play targets better each iteration (**val_acc 0.522 → 0.638**),
   actual strength *fell* (**uniform-vs-heuristic 0.594 → 0.568**; h2h-vs-iter-0
   flat at ~0.49). The two are **anti-correlated** — distilling more of the ReBeL
   search policy makes the model play slightly *worse*. The bottleneck is
   **distillation-target quality**, not the gate.

Net: "loop working e2e" ✅; "self-improving" ❌, now for a well-localized reason.

## 2. Problem 1 — the old promotion gate (saturating absolute + ratchet under noise)

`rebel_orchestrator.py` promoted iff `fixed_gate.passed AND wilson_lower >= best_ever_promoted`,
where `wilson_lower` is the challenger's *absolute* win-rate vs the rules-based
heuristic opponent (`sim-eval-gate` plays MCTS-model vs heuristic). Two failure
modes, both observed:

- **Saturation.** Fixed-deck win-rate clusters ~0.57–0.64 across wildly different
  particle/search/KL settings (R17 0.573–0.642; R18 0.535–0.562). The heuristic
  caps achievable win-rate near ~0.60, so genuine policy gains stop moving the
  number — the improvement signal vanishes.
- **Ratchet under noise.** The `>=` floor has no significance margin and only
  ratchets up. At ~480–960 games the Wilson half-width is ±0.03–0.06, so a model
  statistically *tied* with the incumbent fails ~half the time, and a lucky-high
  promotion locks in a floor later iters can't clear. R17 blocked iters 2–17
  (16 straight non-promotions) despite all passing the absolute bar; R18's
  iter-4 "promotion" was almost certainly a noise blip, not real improvement.

This matches the archived reviewer note "promotion ratchet under noise"
(`docs/archive/ai-research/ai-performance-research-backlog-archive.md`), whose
prescribed fix (confidence-band tolerance) was never implemented — superseded
here by the head-to-head gate.

## 3. Fix — head-to-head challenger-vs-champion gate (commit `430be83`)

The statistically correct test of "did we improve" is: **does the new model beat
the current champion?** This is self-scaling (the opponent strengthens with every
promotion, so no saturation ceiling) and needs no arbitrary ratchet.

Mechanism (4 files, +224/−11):

- **`engine-rs/.../inference/mod.rs`** — added a thread-local session override
  (`set_active_override` + `active()` = override-or-`global()`). `global()`
  unchanged; default behavior byte-for-byte identical for all existing callers.
- **`engine-rs/.../mcts/driver.rs`** — the 3 MCTS model-access sites use
  `inference::active()` instead of `global()`.
- **`engine-rs/.../bin/eval_gate.rs`** — new `--opponent-onnx`: loads a 2nd ONNX
  session and runs the opponent side through `run_mcts` under the override.
  Win attribution / Wilson summary unchanged — challenger is `model_side`, so
  `summary.overall.wilson95.lower` already means "challenger beat champion."
- **`training/rebel_orchestrator.py`** — new `--gate-promote-margin`. The FIXED
  gate runs head-to-head vs `state.promoted_onnx` with `--min-ci-lower = 0.5 +
  margin` when a champion exists; iter-0 bootstraps against the heuristic at
  `--gate-min-ci-lower`. The monotone `wilson_lower >= previous` ratchet was
  **removed**: `promote = gate.passed`. UNIFORM gate stays absolute-vs-heuristic
  as a regression sanity signal. Decision reasons:
  `bootstrap-gate-passed/failed`, `h2h-beat-champion/below-champion`.

**Validation (CPU, 120 games, iter-0/iter-3 of R18):** routing is provably
correct and deterministic — A-vs-B and B-vs-A win-rates are *exactly*
complementary (58/120 vs 62/120) and a model-vs-itself mirror is exactly 60/120.

## 4. R19 result — the null self-improvement finding

Head-to-head gate, p64/s48, KL 0.02, gate 480 games/side, margin 0.0.

| iter | h2h win-rate vs iter-0 | uniform win-rate vs heuristic | final val_acc | decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | — (bootstrap 0.621 vs heuristic) | 0.594 | 0.522 | **promoted → champion** |
| 1 | 0.489 | 0.581 | 0.572 | rejected (h2h-below-champion) |
| 2 | 0.494 | 0.579 | 0.629 | rejected |
| 3 | 0.491 | 0.568 | 0.638 | rejected |

Killed at iter-4 (consec_fail 3/6). **Champion never advanced past iter-0.**

Two robust signals:

- **h2h vs iter-0 is flat at 0.489 / 0.494 / 0.491** — three independent
  960-game measurements clustered within ±0.3% just *below* 50%. Not noise: the
  trained generations are reproducibly even-to-marginally-worse than iter-0. The
  gate correctly refuses to promote them (and was *not* fooled the way the old
  absolute gate was — contrast R18 "promoting" iter-4).
- **val_acc ↑ while strength ↓** — fitting the self-play targets better
  (0.522 → 0.638) tracks *lower* real win-rate (0.594 → 0.568). The distillation
  target is **misaligned with playing strength**: more imitation of the ReBeL
  search policy = slightly weaker play.

## 5. Diagnosis — improvement source, not the gate

The loop's premise is "search > policy, training distills the gain." R19 shows
that pipeline is not delivering a gain, and the val_acc↑/winrate↓ divergence says
the targets themselves are the problem. Compounding it is a **self-play
cold-start**: because nothing beats iter-0, the actor and the entire PFSP pool
stay frozen at iter-0, so every iteration trains on the same iter-0-distribution
data and lands back at ~iter-0. The first improvement is the hardest and the loop
never gets it.

Candidate root causes (unverified, ordered by suspicion):

1. **Search is not stronger than the raw policy at the source.** If iter-0's
   search-driven play does not beat iter-0's raw-policy play, there is no gain to
   distill. This is the decisive check — see next steps.
2. **Value/Q heads corrupting the shared trunk.** `--value-weight 0.2
   --q-value-weight 0.35` on a shared trunk; rising val_acc may be the value/Q
   objectives dragging the policy. A policy-only (value/Q weight 0) iteration
   isolates this.
3. **KL-anchor-to-iter-0 pinning** the learner (init-from-iter-0 + KL anchor +
   gate-vs-iter-0 all point at iter-0), suppressing any move away from it.
4. **Too little data per iter** (768 games, ~5–8k rows) to extract a small
   search edge.

## 6. Next experiments (pre-register in scoping before running)

- **Search-vs-policy strength probe** (root-cause #1): measure iter-0 search-play
  vs iter-0 raw-policy-play head-to-head. Needs per-side sims asymmetry in
  `sim-eval-gate` (small add) or direct policy sampling. Determines whether *any*
  config can self-improve here. Route via
  `docs/ai-research/scoping/per-side-asymmetry-probe-scoping.md`.
- **Policy-only ablation** (#2): one iteration with `--value-weight 0
  --q-value-weight 0`; does h2h-vs-iter-0 rise above 0.5?
- **KL-off ablation** (#3): `--kl-anchor-weight 0`; does the learner move?
- Inspect raw self-play target rows for degenerate/peaked policy labels.

The head-to-head gate is the right instrument for all of these — it gives an
honest, non-saturating improvement signal, so any real gain will now show as a
promotion instead of being lost in heuristic-gate noise.
