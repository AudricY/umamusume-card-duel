## Hypothesis

The R7 plateau (`wl ≤ 0.33` across R7/R8/R7.b.2/mcts-distill-v1) may
be a *corpus-distribution* artifact, not a model-capacity or
architecture limit. Every raw-policy SL training corpus to date
shared three properties that are no longer cost-bound:

1. Generated by a model-in-the-loop policy (teacher BC traces,
   item17-2026-05-11 rollout corpus, or chunks 5a-i 3a relabel
   corpus) — propagates the model's own biases into label
   distributions.
2. Single-matchup distribution (Matikane mirror; deck-pair-sampling
   confirmed every post-R110 measurement was on this distribution).
3. Sized at ~10k-12k decision rows (R7 mixed-teacher corpus = 12,387
   rows; 3a relabel = 11,798 rows) — sample sizes that were
   prohibitive when corpus generation took weeks.

**Distinguishing claims:**

- **Rule-bot vs rule-bot driven.** No model in the corpus-generating
  policy — the data is *state-space expansion* from heuristic AI
  vs heuristic AI play (chunk 5g-style audit-as-source), not a
  model-policy rollout. Eliminates the "labels confidently wrong on
  contested heads" mechanism (chunk 5g Case B) at its root.
- **Uniform deck distribution.** 22/22 player×AI matchups in the
  corpus (deck-pair-sampling Slice 2 default — already wired into
  `sim-export-training`). Eliminates the single-matchup distribution
  collapse.
- **High-sim MCTS relabel for labels.** Re-relabel the rule-bot
  corpus rows with `sims=400` (or 1000) policy targets. Chunk 5i
  argued the argmax-flip from 100→400 sims was 17.3% on the *old*
  3a relabel corpus (model-policy-driven, single-matchup) — and
  killed candidate-1 at that cost. That null is conditional on the
  old corpus; on a rule-bot-driven uniform-deck corpus the
  flip-rate question reopens because the *baseline distribution*
  the model relabels is different.
- **Tens of thousands of games.** Sample size that was prohibitive
  pre-throughput. G5 + Slice 3c put a single rule-bot-vs-rule-bot
  game at ~140-220× the TS engine baseline; 50k-100k games is now
  ~hours at workers=16.

**What this probe tests:** does a fresh raw-policy SL training run
on a rule-bot-driven, uniform-deck, high-sim-MCTS-relabeled corpus at
50k+ games clear the R7.b.3 pre-registered `wl ≥ 0.40` gate, and/or
the R7.b.2 strict-non-regression `wl ≥ 0.30` floor?

## Why now

Two unlocks landed within the last week that this line was
implicitly gated on:

1. **G5 + Slice 3 + Rust path:** `gpu-inference-execution-provider.md`
   G5 (commit `fb7d3f8`) collapsed the CPU `Mutex<Session>` to a
   lock-free `UnsafeCell<Session>`, unlocking 7.11× scaling at
   workers=16 over workers=1. Combined with Slice 1's Rust ort
   in-process path (140-220× over TS engine on rule-bot rollouts)
   and Slice 3's v3.2 featurizer port, a 50k-game rule-bot corpus
   that would have been ~2 weeks of TS engine wallclock is now a
   few hours.
2. **`deck-pair-sampling` Slice 2 default-on uniform selfplay**
   (commit `0e391f3`). The selfplay corpus now covers 22/22
   player×AI matchups by default; `sim-export-training` already
   carries `--deck-sampling=uniform` plumbing. Deck variety is no
   longer a separate engineering line — it's the default
   distribution.

The conjunction "rule-bot × uniform deck × high-sim relabel × tens
of thousands of games × fresh raw-policy SL" is novel under the new
regime. Each axis individually has prior datapoints; the joint
distribution is unmeasured.

**Cost economics (pre-G5 vs post-G5):**

- Pre-G5: 50k rule-bot games × ~7s/game TS engine ≈ 4 days wall on
  one CPU; relabel at 400-sim ≈ another 2 days; raw-policy SL train
  ~30s on CUDA; raw-policy gate at n=1000 ~80s. Total ~6+ days for
  one probe.
- Post-G5: 50k rule-bot games × ~0.03-0.05s/game on Rust + workers=16
  ≈ 25-40 min wall; relabel at 400-sim ≈ 1-2 h via
  `sim-mcts-selfplay` with `--prior policy`; train ~30s; gate ~80s.
  Total ~2-3 h for one probe.

The line was DONE-NEGATIVE on existing-corpus distributions; it has
never been tested on a corpus shaped by the new throughput regime.

## Why this isn't a re-run of chunks 5a-i

Chunks 5a-i exhausted DPO / soft-CE BC / label-quality audit / sim
count on the **existing 11,798-row 3a relabel corpus** — a corpus
generated by the production model's rollout-leaf MCTS at sims=100,
single matchup (`defaultAiOpponentDeckId` silently resolving to
AI-flavor Matikane), on top of an item17-era rollout trajectory.

The specific exhaustions (with their attribution):

- **Chunk 5f — soft-CE BC vs sum-pool baseline:** BC on the 3a
  relabel corpus regressed `pair_accuracy` 0.6981 → 0.6802 (Δ
  −0.0179) — labels actively mislead training. Falsifies candidate-3
  (soft-CE BC fixes the DPO ceiling) on **that corpus**. The
  rule-bot-driven uniform-deck corpus has a fundamentally different
  label distribution (no model-policy bias in the source policy)
  and a fundamentally different state distribution (22 matchups
  vs 1).
- **Chunk 5g — label audit Case B:** 27% of 3a rows have relabel
  confidence ≥ 0.8 yet only 70% agree with the untrained ref on
  contested heads (`pass`/`playTrainer`/`retreatAttack`); mean
  KL(rel‖ref) = 0.331 nats. **Labels confidently wrong** on
  contested heads. The mechanism: rollout-leaf MCTS at 100 sims
  was producing policy targets worse than the untrained ref's own
  policy on contested states. **Rule-bot rollouts as the source
  policy do not have this property** — the rule bot is the *gate
  opponent*, not the labels-trainer; rule-bot mirror games surface
  decision states the model has never seen because the rule bot
  navigates them differently. The audit machinery
  (`runs/R16-TD-3a-prod-corpus/candidate1-killtest/`) can be reused
  on the new corpus as the **gating diagnostic** before any
  training compute.
- **Chunk 5h — sim count ladder scoping:** doc-only scoping for
  the argmax-flip test; flipped step ordering, no corpus impact.
- **Chunk 5i — 400-sim argmax flip:** 17.3% flip rate on the
  contested-head + 100-sim max-prob ≥ 0.8 + state-identical subset
  (n=365) on the **3a relabel corpus**. Killed candidate-1
  (relabel-MCTS too weak vs ref) — but only on that corpus's state
  distribution. A rule-bot-driven uniform-deck corpus has a
  different state distribution (different contested-state mix,
  different deck archetypes' tactical states), so the 17.3% point
  estimate does not transfer.
- **Chunk 5j — n=1000 contested-coverage confirmation gate:**
  FALSIFIED the cheap-slope pilot — Option-1 fixed-retained-count
  contested-coverage reweighting did not move the gate. Closed the
  "reweight the same corpus" axis; the "generate a different
  corpus" axis is exactly what this line proposes.

**Bright-line distinguisher:** If we reused the same 3a corpus and
just retrained a fresh policy on it, the result would be bound by
chunk 5g's "labels confidently wrong" finding. This line MUST
generate a NEW corpus from a different source policy
(rule-bot-vs-rule-bot, not model-rollout) on a different state
distribution (22 matchups, not 1) to be worth testing. The probe is
**a corpus-distribution test**, not a label-shape test on the same
distribution.

featurizer code.

  and `--workers` for parallel relabel.
- `training/train_bc.py` — existing raw-policy SL trainer.
  `--data-mode bc` is the apples-to-apples shape vs R7.b.2 (R4-era
  rollout corpus, schema v3.0); `--data-mode rollout` for soft-CE
  BC on `policyTargets` if Slice 2's first acceptance check
  needs it.
- `training/r8_gate_eval.py` — raw-policy gate via headless
  rule-bot at n=1,000 side-balanced, seed-start 9000. **The same

- `training/uma_ai/dataset.py` — v3.2 schema (Directive B); the
  state representation is the slot-token branch on top of the

## Recipe for first probe (P0 + Slice 1 + Slice 2)

### P0 — corpus-gen smoke (~half day, infra-validating)

Generate 200 rule-bot vs rule-bot games via
`sim-export-training --deck-sampling=uniform --games 200
--seed-start 50000 --workers 8` on the Rust fast path. Validate:

- Per-game manifest emits `playerDeckId` + `opponentDeckId` (already
  required by deck-pair-sampling Slice 1).
- 22/22 deck pairs visible in the manifest aggregate.
- `TrainingExample` JSONL rows pass `training/uma_ai/dataset.py`'s
  loader without OOV warnings.
- No card-data drift panics (overlaps with deck-pair-sampling
  P0b smoke).
- Wallclock: target ≤ 2 min for 200 games at workers=8 (corpus-gen
  proportion of the throughput budget).

**KILL CRITERION:** any of the above fails ⇒ corpus-gen infra is
not ready under the new regime; document gap and pause.

### Slice 1 — full corpus gen + high-sim relabel (~2-3 h wall)

- 50,000 rule-bot vs rule-bot games via `sim-export-training`,
  `--deck-sampling=uniform`, `--workers 16`. Expected wall ~25-40
  min on the Rust fast path.
- Relabel via `sim-mcts-selfplay` (or a relabel-only mode in
  `export_training` if added — pure plumbing) at `--prior policy
  --leaf rollout --sims 400`, consuming the production
  v3.2 lineage best ckpt
  (`runs/v32-uniform-retrain-from-R110-W6/loop/iter-1/policy.onnx`,
  wl=0.5750 at n=240). Expected wall ~1-2 h at workers=16.
- **Label-quality gating diagnostic before training compute:** run
  the chunk 5g audit (top-1 agreement + KL(rel‖ref) + mean entropy
  per kind) on the new corpus against the v3.2 ckpt's own softmax.
  If `top-1 agreement ≤ 0.65 AND p90 max-prob ≥ 0.8` on contested
  heads — the chunk 5g Case B signature — abort before training
  (the new corpus inherited the same "labels confidently wrong"
  property, this distribution change did not fix it).

### Slice 2 — raw-policy SL train + gate (~half day wall)

- `train_bc.py --data-mode bc --hidden 64 --depth 2 --epochs 75
  --state-dim 110` (v3.2 STATE_DIM_V3=110) on the relabeled
  corpus. Same HP as R7.b.2 (apples-to-apples).
- ONNX export, roundtrip smoke (`max_logit_diff < 1e-5`).
- Gate via `training/r8_gate_eval.py --games 500 --seed-start 9000
  --min-ci-lower 0.40` on the fixed-matchup gate (apples-to-apples
  vs R7.b.2 0.3045). Wall ~80 s.
- **Acceptance (primary):** `wilson_lower ≥ 0.40` ⇒ R7.b.3
  pre-registered gate cleared on a corpus-distribution lever.
  Raw-policy SL re-opens as a forward line; the R7 plateau was a
  corpus-quality artifact, not a model-capacity limit.
- **Acceptance (lift-but-cap):** `0.30 ≤ wilson_lower < 0.40` ⇒
  corpus-distribution helps but does not clear F1; document as a
  partial datapoint, do NOT proceed to Slice 3.
- **Falsification:** `wilson_lower < 0.30` ⇒ a fresh
  corpus-distribution does not help raw-policy SL either. Combined
  with the attention-probe outcome (set-attention probe Slice 2
  was the architecture-axis falsification path), the raw-policy
  ceiling is *fundamental at this game-engine quality*. Close the
  line as DONE-NEGATIVE on the corpus-distribution axis.

## Acceptance + falsification criteria

**Pre-training gating (Slice 1, audit-only):**

- Reuse chunk 5g audit shape on the new corpus. If `top-1
  agreement ≥ 0.75 OR p90 max-prob < 0.7` on contested heads
  (`pass`/`playTrainer`/`retreatAttack`), the new corpus is
  *materially distinct* from the 3a corpus's failure mode.
  Proceed to Slice 2.
- If chunk 5g Case B signature reproduces on the new corpus,
  document and pause before spending Slice 2 train compute.

**Training-gate acceptance (Slice 2):**

- **PRIMARY:** `wilson_lower ≥ 0.40` at the R7.b.3 pre-registered
  gate (n=1,000 side-balanced rule-bot, seed-start 9000). Cleanly
  reopens raw-policy SL.
- **LIFT-BUT-CAP:** `0.30 ≤ wilson_lower < 0.40` — partial; do NOT
  proceed to Slice 3.
- **FALSIFICATION:** `wilson_lower < 0.30` — corpus-distribution
  axis closed on the raw-policy SL gate.

**Joint falsification with attention probe:**

If both the attention probe (Slice 2 of
`set-attention-architecture-probe.md`) AND this line's Slice 2
return `wilson_lower < 0.30`, the architectural lever AND the
corpus-distribution lever are both exhausted at the F1 gate. The
raw-policy ceiling is fundamental at the current game-engine
fidelity; close the raw-policy SL frontier permanently and route
all forward effort to search-wrapped lines.

## Out of scope

- New decks. 13 premade decks is the test bed; same as
  deck-pair-sampling.
- Bigger model architectures. R6 closes capacity-from-above; the
  attention probe owns the inductive-bias axis. This line is
  strictly the corpus-distribution lever at the production trunk.
- New features / new schema versions. v3.2 is mandatory
  (Directive B) and sufficient.
- Re-running chunks 5a-i probes (DPO / soft-CE BC / sim-count
  ladder) on the new corpus speculatively — only if the
  pre-training gating diagnostic surfaces a specific mechanism
  that those probes already tested.
- Cross-iter opponent-pool selfplay during corpus gen. The corpus
  is rule-bot-vs-rule-bot only — no model in the loop. Mixing in
  pool selfplay re-introduces model-policy bias into the labels.
- Online vs offline. This is offline corpus-gen → relabel → train.
  No closed-loop iteration on the corpus side.

## Open questions

- **Does the corpus need to be ONLY rule-bot, or rule-bot +
  rule-bot-with-noise?** Pure deterministic rule-bot games may
  collapse to a small state-space at uniform decks (the rule bot
  has no stochastic exploration). Noisy rule-bot or temperature
  sampling on the rule bot's softmax score may be needed for
  state diversity. Cheap to check via per-state hash dedup on the
  P0 smoke.
- **Is 50k games the right size, or is 20k sufficient?** P0 smoke
  emits decision-rows-per-game; the corpus-size point estimate
  for "enough to saturate v3.2 trunk capacity" is unmeasured.
  Default starting size 50k; can be revisited after Slice 1 audit
  diagnostics.
- **Does the relabel ckpt matter?** Slice 1 default is the
  current v3.2 lineage best (wl=0.5750). If a re-verdict-#3
  tight-gate measurement promotes a different v3.2 ckpt, that
  becomes the default relabel ckpt; the line is not
  ckpt-version-bound.
- **Soft-CE BC vs hard-CE BC on the relabeled corpus?** R7.b.2
  used hard-CE BC; chunk 5f tested soft-CE BC on the 3a corpus
  and falsified. The corpus distribution is the lever here, not
  the loss shape; default = hard-CE BC for apples-to-apples vs
  R7.b.2. Soft-CE can be a Slice-2-follow-up if hard-CE clears
  0.40 and a paired-axis sanity check is warranted.
- **Eval gate sample size for Slice 2.** n=1,000 keeps
  apples-to-apples vs R7.b.2; n=10,000 would tighten the
  acceptance verdict by ~3pp but costs ~10× wall. Default n=1,000
  for first probe; n=10,000 only if the n=1,000 verdict sits
  within ±2pp of either acceptance threshold.
